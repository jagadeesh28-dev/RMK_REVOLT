## 2. Mathematical Formulation

The expected net economic value $\mathbb{E}[V]$ generated by qualifying an intake specimen is formulated as:

$$\mathbb{E}[V] = \mathbb{E}[R_{\text{gross}}] - \mathbb{E}[C_{\text{liability}}] - C_{\text{testing}}$$

Where:
$$\mathbb{E}[R_{\text{gross}}] = P_{\text{safe}} \cdot QAR \cdot R_{\text{second-life}} + P_{\text{retired}} \cdot R_{\text{salvage}}$$

$$R_{\text{second-life}} = \mu_{SOH} \cdot E_{\text{nominal}} \cdot N_{\text{cycles}} \cdot \tau_{\text{energy}}$$

$$R_{\text{salvage}} = E_{\text{nominal}} \cdot \tau_{\text{salvage}}$$

$$\mathbb{E}[C_{\text{liability}}] = P_{\text{unsafe}} \cdot FAR \cdot C_{\text{hazard}}$$

$$C_{\text{testing}} = C_{\text{handling}} + \left(E_{\text{diag}} \cdot \tau_{\text{energy}}\right) + \frac{C_{\text{capex}}}{N_{\text{asset-life}}}$$

### Variables & Definitions
- $P_{\text{safe}}, P_{\text{unsafe}}$: Intake population prevalence ($41.7\%$ safe, $58.3\%$ unsafe from retirement fleet).
- $QAR$: Qualified Acceptance Rate among truly safe cells.
- $FAR$: False Acceptance Rate among truly unsafe cells.
- $E_{\text{nominal}}$: Nominal energy capacity ($0.064\text{ kWh}$ per cell, $0.64\text{ kWh}$ per 10S module).
- $N_{\text{cycles}}$: Effective usable cycle life in second-life BESS duty.
- $\tau_{\text{energy}}$: Grid arbitrage electricity tariff (₹/kWh).
- $\tau_{\text{salvage}}$: Hydro-metallurgical black mass scrap tariff (₹/kWh).
- $C_{\text{hazard}}$: Financial liability penalty per field thermal incident/failure.
- $C_{\text{testing}}$: Amortized test bench depreciation and electrical running cost.

## 7. Model Boundary Conditions & Failure Modes `[THEORETICAL]`

The economic model holds strictly under the following physical and commercial boundary conditions:

1. **Intake Quality Threshold:** If the intake battery population has safe prevalence $P_{\text{safe}} < 10\%$, salvage scrap value dominates. Qualification becomes uneconomical if intake transport costs exceed ₹50/module.
2. **Commodity Scrap Collapse:** If black mass recycling tariffs drop below ₹400/kWh, rejected batteries become an environmental liability cost rather than an asset.
3. **Hardware Contact Wear:** The economic model assumes Kelvin clip contactor maintenance every 25,000 cycles (cost: ₹5,000 per fixture replacement).
4. **Chemistry Homogeneity:** The model assumes the facility is configured for 12.8V / 24V LFP second-life stationary storage. If a module cannot be classified as LFP with high confidence ($P(\text{LFP}) < 99\%$), it is automatically diverted to hydro-metallurgical recycling at scrap tariff $\tau_{\text{salvage}}$ rather than sold as a second-life asset.
